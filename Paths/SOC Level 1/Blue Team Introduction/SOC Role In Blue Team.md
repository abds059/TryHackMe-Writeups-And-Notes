## Task 2 - Security Hierarchy

The cybersecurity needs and priorities are different for each company. For law firms, it is the protection of data, for manufacturing companies the availability of production lines, for hospitals it is the patient's safety. Because of this every company differs from the other based on security approach and the team structure. However a general structure is as follows:

![Security Hierarchy](../../../IMAGES/Paths/SOC%20Level%201/Blue%20Team%20Introduction/SOC%20Role%20In%20Blue%20Team/Security%20Hierarchy.png)

Looking at the diagram, the top level executives like the CEO usually arent involved technical aspects and focus more on business goals. That's why they may hire a Chief Information Secuity Officer (CISO) to handle the technical stuff.

### Security Departments

In very small companies the IT department takes the role of managing security as well. However, for small to medium sized companies a generic Information Security team may handle all these stuff. But for large size comapanies, there is usually a CISO overseeing multiple security teams each with different purpose like:

- **Red Team:** Offensive security experts, pentesters, or ethical hackers who look for security issues
- **GRC Team:** Specialists managing policies and ensuring compliance with regulations like PCI DSS
- **Blue Team:** Defensive security experts like SOC analysts, engineers, or incident responders

## Task 3 - Meet The Blue Team

Blue team is all about defensive security, constantly defending against attacks and resposnding to them quickly. Depending on company's size, Blue team can include a lot of roles and sub departments. Some of the common Blue team departments are discussed below.

### Security Operations Center (SOC)

SOC  is the central hub for a organization's cybersecurity, as they act as the first line of defense against threats. A SOC usually consists of the following roles:

- **L1 Analysts** - Junior members who triage alerts and excalate complex cases to L2
- **L2 Analysts** - Experienced members who investigate advanced alerts
- **Engineers** - Experts in configuring security tools like EDR or SIEM
- **Manager** - A person responsible for managing the whole SOC team

### Cyber Incident Respone Team (CIRT)

If a SOC team is not enough to handle a threat or the incident goes out of control, CIRT (also called CSIRT or CERT), comes into action. The members of these teams possess broad knowledge of cyber threats and handle breaches without tools like EDR and SIEM. A few examples are:

- **JPCERT** - Japan's CERT handling nation-wide breaches
- **Mandiant** - A private team responding to global cyber incidents
- **AWS CIRT** - Investigates security incidents of AWS customers

### Specialized Defensive Roles

Large companies and government agencies often need narrow and specialized blue team roles that require deep knowledge and broad experience in fields like SOC or IT. These roles include:

- **Digital Forensics Analyst** - Uncover hidden threats in disk and memory
- **Threat Intelligence Analyst** - Gather data about emerging threat groups
- **Appsec Engineer** - Maintain a secure sofware development lifecycle
- **AI Researcher** - Study AI threats and how to defend against them

### Answer the questions below

- Does Blue Team focus on defensive or offensive security?

    `Defensive`

- Which department handles active or urgent cyber incidents?

    `CIRT`

## Task 4 - Advancing SOC Path

### SOC Path

Initially starting out in cybersecurity as a SOC L1 Analyst can be a great option as it will broaden cybersecurity world awareness and help in better understanding more specialized roles. Also, entry level SOC L1 roles can be fun and engaging as we get a chance to deal with real attacks and learna lot through the process. Some good starting points for this role can be:

- Gain core SOC skills and practice them. Related skills like red teaming or general IT would help, too!
- Be proactive, try yourself in CTFs, stay in the loop of cyber news, and consider the SAL1 certification!
- Prepare for an interview, learn the difference between an internal SOC and MSSP, and apply for a job!
- After working for some time in a junior position, consider preparing and advancing to more senior roles!

### Internal SOC vs MSSP

Many organization dont have the relevant expertise to operate a SCO on their own so they rely on Managed Security Services Provider (MSSP) for this task. An MSSP delivers security services like SOC services to its clients. Working there is a high pressure work but its also a good option to start. 

### Next Steps

The most natural steps after L1 is to progress to L2 position but its not necessary as one can go into work that interests that person the most like CIRT or SOC Engineer position. 

### Answer the questions below

- How would you call a cyber security company providing SOC services?

    `MSSP`

- Which role naturally continues your SOC L1 analyst journey?

    `SOC L2 Analyst`
